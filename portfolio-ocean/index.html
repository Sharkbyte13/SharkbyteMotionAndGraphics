The Background

Physics by day,
pixels by choice.

Earning a Bachelor of Science in Astrophysics at UCLA meant spending years inside data — astronomical observations, photometric reduction pipelines, Python-based analysis — hunting for signal in enormous amounts of noise. Research experience in quantum physics added a second layer: the discipline of working at the edge of what is currently understood, where the methods matter as much as the findings.

Design was never the detour. It was the other current running underneath. Both disciplines ask the same fundamental question: how do you take something complex and render it clear?

Research

Observational astronomy
& quantum systems.

Undergraduate research at UCLA spanned two fronts: hands-on observational astronomy — nights at the telescope, photometric reduction, data pipelines — and theoretical work touching the edges of quantum mechanics, where the rules that govern matter at the smallest scale start bending in ways that will reshape every industry over the coming decades.

The Connection

Why it makes the
design sharper.

Physics trains you to distrust shortcuts. When you've spent time verifying that a signal is real and not an artifact of your reduction pipeline, you develop a healthy skepticism of the first answer — in science and in design. The first layout that "works" is rarely the optimal one. The first color that looks fine is rarely the right one.

A curious mindset combined with analytical strategy: that's the toolkit. One discipline informs the other, and the result is design that doesn't just look good — it holds up under scrutiny.
